What this is. A monitoring feed for the space front: the launch industry, orbital infrastructure, military use of orbit, the money moving through it, the law being written around it, and the land it is built on. Nothing here is written or summarised — headlines and snippets are the publishers' own, every row links to the original, and no model touches the text.

How a story gets in. It has to name something unambiguously about spaceflight — a rocket, a satellite, a spaceport, an agency, a company, a treaty. Words that only sometimes mean space (launch, mission, orbit, constellation, debris) never qualify a story on their own, which is what keeps product launches and football fixtures out. Each story is then tagged with every subject it matches, so a military payload on a commercial rocket appears under both.

Watchdog wires. Trade press covers contracts and countdowns closely and covers protests, lawsuits, environmental reviews, subsidies and land disputes thinly. Eight searches run specifically for those, and everything they return is tagged Protest & accountability and marked in the list. Filter to that subject to see the front as an activist rather than an investor.

Translation. The Translate control at the top renders the headlines and snippets on screen into any of 26 languages, through a free public translation endpoint — no account, no key, nothing stored. A translated row is marked as machine translation and keeps the publisher's original wording on hover, because a machine rendering is not the publisher's words. If the endpoint is unavailable the row simply stays in its original language rather than emptying. Translation applies to what is on screen; scroll further or widen a filter and press the control again.

Limits worth knowing. Google News caps a query at roughly 100 results over about 30 days, so a heavy month in one language gets truncated. A regional edition often carries a syndicated wire story rather than local reporting, which inflates a language count without adding a local view. And coverage is uneven by design of the media, not of this feed: expect Swahili, Bengali and Persian to read near zero most days. The counts are shown rather than hidden.
